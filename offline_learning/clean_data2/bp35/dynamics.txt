bp35 (bp35-0a0ad940) — game mechanics

OVERVIEW
- A side-view GRAVITY PLATFORMER / falling-block puzzle. You control a small
  player sprite that can only move horizontally (left / right); a constant
  downward gravity makes it fall whenever it steps off a ledge. The goal of each
  level is to reach the GEM. You shape the path down by CLICKING removable
  green blocks (delete them so the player can drop through). ACTION7 UNDOes the
  last move. There are 9 levels; clearing a level loads the next and resets the
  per-level move budget.

GRID & LAYERS / CAMERA
- The emitted observation is a single 64x64, 16-color layer (one <grid_N>).
- The actual playfield is an 11-cell-wide level laid out vertically (level 1 is
  ~36 cells tall). Each game cell is rendered as a 6x6 pixel block, so only a
  vertical slice of the level is visible at a time. A CAMERA scrolls vertically
  to follow the player (camera y = player_grid_y*6 - 36 at level start, then it
  pans with each fall). Because of this scroll the same world cell appears at
  different screen rows over time — this matters for clicks (see CLICK).
- The very bottom screen row (pixel row 63) is a MOVE-BUDGET / progress bar, not
  part of the playfield. It fills left-to-right as you spend actions.

COORDINATES
- World cells are (gx = col, gy = row). gx grows rightward. gy grows UPWARD in
  world space, BUT gravity pulls toward DECREASING gy, i.e. "down on screen" =
  -gy. So the player falls from high gy toward low gy; the gem sits below
  (lower gy than) the start.
- On screen, larger pixel-y is lower. The renderer flips world gy to screen so
  that falling looks like moving downward.

PALETTE (16-color indices actually used)
-  0 = black: player eye accent / spike accent.
-  3 = dark (PADDING_COLOR): small highlight dots inside walls and on blocks.
-  5 = light cyan: WALL / solid terrain (xcjjwqfzjfe). This is the floor/ceiling
       you stand on and cannot pass.
-  7 = pink: the GEM (fjlzdjxhant) — the level goal.
-  9 = blue: PLAYER body. (sprite "player_right"/"player_left"; the facing eye
       accent uses color 11, so the player reads as a blue blob with a yellow
       eye that points in the last-moved direction.)
- 10 = background (BACKGROUND_COLOR): the dotted dark empty space (air).
- 11 = player eye / spike body.
- 14 = green: CLICKABLE REMOVABLE BLOCK (qclfkhjnaac) — the main puzzle element
       in levels 1-2. Clicking one deletes it.
- 15 = the move-budget bar fill, and the body of spikes / spawn blocks.
- Later levels add other clickable types (see CLICKABLE TYPES) that render in
  other colors (8 = gravity-flip block, 12 = toggle block, 15 = spawn block).

OBJECTS (by internal name)
- player_right / player_left  : the agent. One per level. Faces the last
  horizontal move direction.
- xcjjwqfzjfe  : solid WALL. Blocks movement; you stand/land on it. Not clickable.
- fjlzdjxhant  : GEM (goal). Moving into it, or falling onto it, WINS the level.
- qclfkhjnaac  : green REMOVABLE block. Solid (you stand on it / it blocks you)
  until you CLICK it, which deletes it. The only clickable in levels 1-2.
- ubhhgljbnpu / hzusueifitk : SPIKES. Falling onto / landing on one LOSES the
  level. (Present in level 2+; level 1 has no spikes.)
- aknlbboysnc / jcyhkseuorf : a "crusher" / moving hazard pair used in early
  levels; in levels <=3 it advances on a 2-step cadence and a lethal alignment
  with the player loses. (Not encountered on the level-1 paths below.)

PLAYER ACTIONS (only ACTION3, ACTION4, ACTION6, ACTION7 are ever offered)
- ACTION3 = MOVE LEFT  (dx = -1).
- ACTION4 = MOVE RIGHT (dx = +1).
  Horizontal move semantics (one cell):
    * If the destination cell is the GEM  -> move onto it and WIN.
    * If the destination cell is empty/air -> move there, then apply GRAVITY:
      keep falling (gy toward the gravity direction) until the next cell is
      solid. Land on the last air cell. If the cell that stopped the fall is a
      GEM -> WIN; if it is a SPIKE -> LOSE.
    * If the destination cell is SOLID (wall or an un-removed green block) ->
      the move is BLOCKED: the player does a small bump animation and stays put.
    * Moving left past column 0 is blocked (left edge acts as a wall).
  There is no separate "fall" action — gravity is resolved as part of each move.
- ACTION6 = CLICK (carries x,y screen-pixel coords) — see CLICK semantics.
- ACTION7 = UNDO. Restores the state saved before the previous move (player
  position AND any block removals are reverted one step). Undo itself counts as
  an action against the move budget. Repeated ACTION7 walks back multiple steps.
- (ACTION1 up, ACTION2 down, ACTION5 exist in the code but are NOT in the
  available-actions set, so they are never selectable and do nothing if sent.)

CLICK semantics (ACTION6, the `click_X_Y` CLI form -> ACTION6 x=X y=Y)
- Coordinate convention: X = screen COLUMN pixel, Y = screen ROW pixel, both in
  0..63 of the rendered frame. The engine converts a click to a world cell with
    world_gx = X // 6
    world_gy = (Y + camera_y) // 6
  i.e. you must account for the current vertical camera scroll. For a target
  world cell (gx,gy), a click at screen (X = gx*6 + 3, Y = gy*6 + 3 - camera_y)
  lands on it. (camera_y is the current camera row offset; it changes as the
  player falls.)
- Clicking a GREEN qclfkhjnaac block DELETES that block. Then, if the deleted
  block was the cell DIRECTLY BELOW the player in the gravity direction, the
  player immediately falls (and may land on a gem -> win, or a spike -> lose).
  If the deleted block was NOT directly under the player, it is simply removed
  (opening a gap the player can later walk/fall into) and the player does not
  move.
- Clicking empty space, a wall, or the player does nothing (still counts as an
  action toward the move budget).

CLICKABLE TYPES (full set across the 9 levels; only green appears in lvls 1-2)
- green  (qclfkhjnaac)  : delete the block.
- blue-8 (lrpkmzabbfa)  : FLIP GRAVITY (toggles fall direction) then resettle.
- cyan-12 toggle pair (yuuqpmlxorv <-> oonshderxef): clicking swaps a block to
  its counterpart (solid <-> passable marker), changing collision.
- white-15 (etlsaqqtjvn) : SPAWN — fills empty orthogonal neighbors with new
  blocks.

AVAILABLE-ACTION TRANSITIONS
- In level 1 the available set is constant: [ACTION3, ACTION4, ACTION6, ACTION7]
  on every step (move left/right, click, undo). It does not change with state.

LEVEL / GOAL STRUCTURE
- 9 levels. Each level is its own vertical map with one player and one gem.
- Level 1 (the map used by the trajectories below): player starts at world cell
  (gx=3, gy=23) on a top ledge; the gem is at (gx=3, gy=7) in a bottom chamber.
  The top floor (gy=22) has a single gap at gx=7. The bottom chamber (the gem's
  room, gy=7..8, floored by wall at gy=6) is sealed above by wall except for a
  3-wide span of GREEN blocks at gy=9, gx=5,6,7 — so the ONLY way into the gem
  room is to remove one of those green blocks and drop through. Reaching it
  requires descending a green-block maze by deleting blocks.
- Clearing a level: on WIN the game advances to the next level (`next_level`),
  loads a fresh map, and RESETS the per-level move counter to 0. The episode
  does not terminate on a single level win (more levels remain); the win shows
  up as a +0.1 reward and a brand-new level layout in the next observation.

WIN / LOSE / SCORE / REWARD
- WIN a level: move into the gem cell, or fall and land on the gem. Grants a
  small reward (observed reward = +0.1 on the transition) and advances to the
  next level. Winning all 9 levels finishes the game.
- LOSE a level: fall onto / land on a SPIKE; be caught by the crusher in early
  levels; OR run out of the MOVE BUDGET. The budget is the bottom progress bar:
  on levels 1-6 you lose at 64 actions; on later levels the cap is larger
  (the bar wraps, ~128 on the mid block, up to ~192 on the final level).
- Score/reward is otherwise 0 per step; only level completion pays out.

EMPIRICAL NOTES / CAVEATS
- All movement and gravity are resolved instantly per action (no passive clock,
  no noop needed). One action -> one settled observation.
- Falls animate the camera; only the final settled frame is emitted, so a long
  fall still produces exactly one observation.
- Because clicks are specified in SCREEN pixels but resolve against a SCROLLING
  camera, the same world block has different click coordinates at different
  points in a trajectory. The exact click_X_Y strings used below were computed
  from the live camera_y at each step.
