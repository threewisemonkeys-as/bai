vc33 (vc33-5430563c) — game mechanics

OVERVIEW
- A click-only gravity / sliding puzzle. The agent never has any movement or
  shoot actions: the only available action is ACTION6 (a click that carries an
  x,y), in EVERY state of EVERY level (the game declares available_actions=[6]).
- Goal of each level: slide every coloured "ball" so that it lines up with the
  matching-coloured "goal" marker. When all balls are aligned the level is
  solved and the game advances to the next level. There are 7 levels
  (win_levels=7). Solving all 7 wins the game.
- A per-level STEP BUDGET (a countdown bar along the very top row) limits how
  many clicks you may spend. Run out before solving the current level and you
  LOSE (episode terminates).

GRID & LAYERS
- One grid layer per observation, always rendered as 64x64 (shape 64x64).
- Each Level has its own logical grid_size, scaled to fill the 64x64 display:
    Level 1: 32x32   Level 2: 32x32   Level 3: 52x52
    Levels 4-7: 64x64
  For a 32x32 level the display is exactly 2x the logical grid (display_col =
  2*grid_col). The click handler converts the clicked DISPLAY coordinate back to
  the logical grid internally, so you always click in DISPLAY (64x64) coords.
- COORDINATE CONVENTION: `click_X_Y` -> ACTION6 with x=X (COLUMN), y=Y (ROW), in
  the 64x64 display frame. (col first, then row.)

PALETTE (colour index -> what it is / how it renders here)
- 0  : interior of a "container" region (the open chambers). Renders white.
- 3  : background / empty space (Camera background). Renders gray. Most common.
- 4  : the ball's "cap" pixel, the little direction arrows drawn over clickable
       bridge blocks, and the screen letter-box padding. Renders dark gray.
- 5  : solid WALLS / floors. Renders black.
- 7  : the STEP-BUDGET bar drawn across the top row (row 0). Renders pink.
- 9  : clickable BUTTONS. Renders blue.
- 11 : a ball+goal colour (yellow).            } a ball only counts as solved
- 14 : a ball+goal colour (green).             } when it reaches a goal marker
- 15 : a ball+goal colour (third colour).      } of the SAME colour.
- 1  : default colour of the clickable bridge blocks in later levels (see
       SECOND MECHANIC). 12 is the recoloured "settled/valid" bridge colour.

OBJECTS (by tag)
- BALL   (tag 0016uciqlhjlom): the piece you move. A small sprite whose body is
  colour 11/14/15 with a colour-4 cap. Its body colour must be matched to a
  same-colour goal. Levels can have one or several balls.
- GOAL   (tag 0010gnulkywfpz): a small colour-11/14/15 marker mounted on a wall.
  It is the target slot for the same-coloured ball. Goals never move.
- BUTTON (tag 0022jvmlspyigc, also sys_click): a small colour-9 (blue) square,
  2x2 or 3x3. Clicking it slides a ball one step (see BUTTON MECHANIC). Buttons
  sit at the edges of the chambers; several may be present per level.
- WALL   (tag 0025yfyiswdvoh): colour-5 black walls / floors that partition the
  board into chambers and that goals are mounted on.
- CONTAINER (tag 0043nzrtobajqi): colour-0 open chambers. A ball "rests on" the
  container under it; clicking a button resizes the two containers a ball is
  passing between (this is the visible chamber-to-chamber transfer).
- BRIDGE BLOCK (tag 0004sttgkofqwb, also sys_click) + ARROW indicators
  (tag 0007gyluczquhi): the colour-1 clickable blocks used only in the later
  levels (see SECOND MECHANIC).

STEP BUDGET (top bar)
- Each level sets a StepCounter (L1=50, L2=50, L3=75, L4=50, L5=200, L6=50,
  L7=200). The top row (row 0) is filled with colour 7 proportional to budget
  remaining and shrinks left-to-right as the budget is spent.
- EVERY ACTION6 click costs exactly 1 step, whether or not it lands on anything
  useful (clicking empty space, a wall, or an inactive button still burns a
  step). Verified empirically: each click decremented the bar by one.
- Exception: clicks made WHILE a bridge animation is playing (later levels) are
  swallowed and do not advance the puzzle (they just let the animation finish).
- The budget is refilled to the new level's StepCounter on every level
  transition.

BUTTON MECHANIC (the core mechanic, levels 1-3 are built entirely on it)
- Every level carries a fixed GRAVITY vector (a direction + magnitude):
    L1 [2,0] right    L2 [-2,0] left   L3 [0,2] down    L4 [0,3] down
    L5 [3,0] right    L6 [-3,0] left   L7 [0,-2] up
  Movement is always ALONG this axis; the magnitude is how far a ball jumps per
  click (2 in L1-L3, 3 in L4-L6, 2 in L7).
- Each BUTTON is paired (at level setup) with a "door" between two adjacent
  chambers. Clicking that button slides the ball that is sitting in one of those
  chambers by exactly the gravity magnitude, through the door into the adjacent
  chamber, and resizes the two chambers accordingly. Different buttons drive the
  ball in opposite directions along the gravity axis.
  Example (Level 1, gravity right, ball starts at grid x=23):
    * clicking the TOP button (display 60,25) moves the ball +2 in x (to the
      right, with gravity): 23 -> 25.
    * clicking the BOTTOM button (display 60,33) moves the ball -2 in x (left):
      23 -> 21.
- ONLY a button whose door is adjacent to the ball's CURRENT chamber does
  anything; clicking a button for a chamber the ball is not in is a no-op (but
  still costs a step). As the ball advances through the chambers, the set of
  "active" buttons changes. This is what makes the multi-chamber levels a maze.
- A ball can only travel along the gravity axis and can be blocked by a wall at
  the end of a chamber's floor (it then just oscillates between the last two
  reachable positions until you take a different door). In Level 2, for example,
  the ball slides x = 4 -> 6 -> 8 -> 10 and then stalls at 10, blocked short of
  its goal column (14) until a different door is used.

WIN CHECK (when does a level complete)
- After every action the game runs the solve test. A level is solved when, for
  EVERY ball, there exists a goal such that:
    (a) the ball's body colour appears in that goal (colour match), AND
    (b) the ball's coordinate ALONG the gravity axis equals the goal's (i.e. for
        horizontal gravity, ball.x == goal.x; for vertical gravity,
        ball.y == goal.y), AND
    (c) the wall the goal is mounted on is adjacent to the ball's chamber.
  Verified in Level 1: the level completed the instant the ball reached x=17,
  the goal's column. (The off-axis coordinate need not match exactly.)
- On solve, next_level() fires: the board switches to the next level and the
  step budget refills. A small positive reward (+0.1) is delivered on the
  transition (reward is 0.0 on all other clicks). The episode does NOT terminate
  on a level transition; it terminates only on the final win or on a loss.

SECOND MECHANIC — BRIDGE BLOCKS (levels 4-7 only; from source, not exercised
in these trajectories)
- Later levels add clickable colour-1 BRIDGE BLOCKS (tag 0004sttgkofqwb) with
  colour-4 ARROW indicators drawn on the ones that are currently actionable.
- Clicking a bridge block that is "valid" (it spans between two chambers — the
  ezbubuphlm test) starts a multi-step ANIMATION: the block swings/repositions
  and carries any balls resting on it across to the adjacent chamber, settling
  over several ticks. While the animation plays, further clicks are ignored
  (and do not cost budget) until it finishes; arrow indicators are hidden during
  the swing and recomputed (recoloured to 12) when it settles. This is the
  alternate way of transferring a ball between chambers in the harder levels,
  layered on top of the button mechanic.

LOSE / TERMINATION / REWARD
- LOSE: if the step budget reaches 0 without the current level being solved, the
  game calls lose() and the episode terminates.
- WIN: solving level 7 (the last level) ends the game as a win.
- REWARD: 0.0 on ordinary clicks; +0.1 on each level transition. No other
  reward signal.
- There is no passive clock: state only changes on a click (and on the
  intermediate ticks of a bridge-block animation). No noop padding is possible
  or meaningful.

CAVEATS
- Levels 2+ are genuine multi-chamber mazes; reaching a goal requires choosing
  the correct door (button) sequence, and some buttons are decoys / only become
  active once the ball is in the right chamber. The trajectories here fully
  solve Level 1 and demonstrate the Level-2 chamber-transfer mechanic (the ball
  was deliberately not driven to the Level-2 goal — see test trajectory notes).
- The SECOND MECHANIC (bridge blocks) is described from the source only; it is
  not reachable without first solving levels 1-3, so it is not shown in the
  train/test frames.
