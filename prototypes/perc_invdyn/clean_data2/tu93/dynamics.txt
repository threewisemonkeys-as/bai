tu93 (tu93-0768757b) — game mechanics

OVERVIEW
- A Pac-Man / maze-navigation game. You steer a single player token through a
  fixed maze to reach the exit before a step-counter runs out. There are 9
  levels of increasing difficulty; clearing the exit advances to the next level.
  Later levels add charging "block" hazards and patrolling enemies.
- This file is reverse-engineered from environment_files/tu93/0768757b/tu93.py
  and confirmed empirically (offline). Level 1 has NO enemies and is fully
  verified frame-by-frame; the enemy rules (levels 2-9) are read from source.

GRID & LAYERS
- Rendered frame is 64x64, 16-color palette. A single grid layer is emitted.
- The level's logical board is smaller (grid_size 39..51 per level) and is
  centered in the 64x64 frame; the surround is letter-box padding.
- The bottom-most frame row (row 63) is a STATUS BAR, not part of the maze.

PALETTE (as actually rendered)
- 5  = black  : maze walls / background / letter-box padding (non-traversable).
- 2  = white  : corridor cell (traversable path).
- 0  = gray   : corridor cell (traversable path). Corridors render as an
                alternating white(2)/gray(0) checkerboard of 3x3 squares; both
                colors are floor the player slides over.
- 9  = blue   : the PLAYER token (a 3x3 square; its top-center pixel is a
                darker notch, color 4).
- 14 = green  : the EXIT cell (a solid 3x3 square). Reaching it clears the level.
- 6  = magenta: the STEP-COUNTER bar drawn along frame row 63 (see STATUS BAR).
- 8 / 12 / 13 : enemy/hazard body colors (levels 2-9; see ENEMIES). When a
                hazard "arms", its top-center pixel turns red (color 11);
                otherwise it is white (15).

UNITS / GEOMETRY
- Everything is built from 3-pixel blocks. A logical maze CELL is 6 pixels
  (the player rests on positions that are multiples of 6 relative to the board).
- The player occupies one 3x3 square but moves a full 6-pixel cell per action.
- A move from a cell to the neighbor cell is allowed only if the 3-pixel
  "bridge" block between them is white (board pixel == 2). If there is no
  bridge (black wall), the action is a NO-OP: the player does not move.

ACTIONS (available_actions = [1,2,3,4] in EVERY state)
- ACTION1 = move UP    (toward smaller row).
- ACTION2 = move DOWN  (toward larger row).
- ACTION3 = move LEFT  (toward smaller column).
- ACTION4 = move RIGHT (toward larger column).
  The chosen direction sets the player's facing/rotation and slides it one cell
  if a path exists; otherwise nothing moves. The four actions are ALWAYS in the
  `avail` list (state-independent) — blocked directions are simply no-ops.
- There is no ACTION5/6/7; this game has NO click action.

CLICK semantics
- Not applicable. ACTION6 is never offered. (Convention reminder for the
  dataset: click_X_Y -> ACTION6 x=X y=Y, x=col,y=row — unused here.)

STATUS BAR / STEP COUNTER
- Each level starts with a StepCounter budget (Level 1=50, others 20..60).
- EVERY action consumes exactly one step, whether or not the player actually
  moved (a blocked no-op still costs a step).
- The magenta bar on row 63 shrinks proportionally to steps_remaining/budget;
  it visibly loses a chunk from the right as the counter decrements, and refills
  to full when a new level loads.
- If the counter hits 0 the player LOSES (episode ends, term=True, reward 0).

LEVEL / GOAL STRUCTURE
- Win condition for a level: get the player token onto the green exit cell
  (player x,y == exit x,y). This triggers next_level().
- Clearing a level gives reward = +0.1 (1/9 of the game) and loads the next
  maze; term stays False until the last level. Clearing all 9 levels wins the
  episode. Empirically: Level-1 exit fires reward=0.1, term=False, and the
  level-2 maze loads on the next frame.
- LOSE (term=True, reward 0) if the step counter reaches 0, or if the player
  token is destroyed by a hazard (see ENEMIES).

LEVEL 1 (verified, enemy-free)
- 6x6 cell maze. Player starts top-left cell; green exit is bottom-right.
- Pure navigation: thread the corridors to the exit within 50 steps. At the
  start only RIGHT (ACTION4) is unblocked; the maze branches and has dead-ends
  further in, but the approach to the exit funnels through forced bottlenecks.

ENEMIES / HAZARDS (levels 2-9; from source, not needed for Level 1)
- Three hazard families, each a 3x3 token sitting on a corridor cell with a
  fixed initial facing (rotation):
  * "block" hazards  (tag 0001haidilggfh, body color 8; also the blue 0000):
    DORMANT until the player lines up with the block along the block's facing
    direction at range 2 cells. Then it ARMS (top-center pixel turns red=11)
    and CHARGES one cell per tick in its facing direction. A charging block
    that reaches the player's cell DESTROYS the player -> you LOSE. (Empirically
    on Level 2: walking straight down the player's row into block 0018 armed it,
    it charged left into the player, and the episode terminated as a loss.)
  * "patroller" enemies (tag 0020npxxteirsg, body color 12): move continuously
    through the maze, bouncing/reversing at walls; contact destroys the player.
  * "armed-walker" enemies (tag 0023otenflmryc, body color 13): once armed they
    walk and, when aligned with the player, destroy it.
- All hazards kill on contact via the same "consume the player token" path
  (the player sprite is removed -> no player remains -> lose()). They are slow,
  one-cell-per-tick movers; the intended play is to bait/dodge their lines of
  motion. A clean script for levels 2-9 must avoid stepping into a hazard's
  charge/patrol line.

COORDINATE CONVENTION (this dataset)
- Movement only; positions are cell-based. left=ACTION3 (-col), right=ACTION4
  (+col), up=ACTION1 (-row), down=ACTION2 (+row).

CAVEATS
- `avail` is constant [ACTION1..ACTION4]; do not infer walls from it. Blocked
  moves are silent no-ops that still cost a step.
- A single action plays out as a short multi-tick slide animation internally;
  only the settled frame is rendered (expected).
- Reward is sparse: 0 every step except +0.1 on each level clear.
