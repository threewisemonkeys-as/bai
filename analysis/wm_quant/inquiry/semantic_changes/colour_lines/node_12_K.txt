The world is a 10x10 grid (rows 0-9, cols 0-9) with a black background. Colored cells (red, green, blue, purple, yellow) appear at integer coordinates. Some cells are "active" and move autonomously one step per time step in a current cardinal direction (up, down, left, or right). Active cells and their directions are determined by the history of clicks. A noop action does not change any direction; it only allows active cells to advance one step in their current direction. A click action (r, c) has the following effects:
- It selects the active cell (usually the one that moved most recently or the one located at the click if present) and immediately changes its direction so that it points towards the clicked cell (i.e., the cell will move one step towards the click on the next frame). The selection rule is: if a cell exists at (r, c) it is the target; otherwise the nearest active cell (by Manhattan distance) of the moving color is affected.
- It also causes an instantaneous movement of that cell by one step in the new direction (so the state changes immediately).
- Additionally, a new cell of a fixed color (consistent for the entire run) is spawned at a location that is predetermined by the click coordinates and the current state; this spawn location can be inferred from the pattern of earlier clicks.
All dynamics are deterministic given the full set of cells and their implicit directions (which are encoded in the spatial history of the active cell’s positions). For inverse dynamics, the correct click is the one that transforms the current cell set into the observed next cell set according to these rules.
